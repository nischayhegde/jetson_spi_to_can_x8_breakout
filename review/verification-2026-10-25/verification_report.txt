1. Findings and required resolution
-----------------------------------

F01 - HIGH: Jetson SPI receive timing has no demonstrated margin at 16-17 MHz.

The carrier routes the SPI header through TXB0108 level translators [3,4].
At 1.8 V to 3.3 V, TI specifies up to 6.8 ns A-to-B and 7.6 ns B-to-A
propagation delay. The MCP251863 specifies up to 20 ns SCK-to-SDO [1, Table
9-7]. A conventional master sampling half a clock after launching the falling
edge therefore has a first-order external path of:

  6.8 + 20 + 7.6 = 34.4 ns,

before carrier/PCB/cable flight time, loading, host setup time and margin.
At 16 MHz the half-period is 31.25 ns; at 17 MHz it is 29.41 ns. Even the
idealized zero-wiring/setup ceiling is 1/(2*34.4 ns) = 14.53 MHz. This is a
worst-case timing-budget concern, not a measurement of this board's failure.
It assumes the expected 1.8/3.3 V translator arrangement and ordinary
half-cycle sampling. The actual Jetson sampling position must be established.

The subsequently specified 5 cm cable is favorable for flight time. At an
illustrative propagation speed of 2e8 m/s, SCK out plus MISO return adds about
0.5 ns; this does not cancel the 34.4 ns device/translator delay above. Cable
construction, capacitance and return conductors remain relevant even at 5 cm.

TI also limits the lumped load driven by the translator to 70 pF and calls
for short interconnects relative to its roughly 10 ns edge-accelerator pulse
[4]. Sum all four controller pin loads, copper, header, carrier and cable.
This total was not established from an IBIS model or measurement. Reducing
the clock rate increases timing margin but does not remove fast-edge
reflections or guarantee an excessive capacitive load will be driven properly.

Resolve with the exact carrier revision, JetPack SPI driver, actual clock,
RX sample delay and load. The inspected upstream Tegra driver has RX/TX clock
tap settings [10], but tap duration and support in the installed kernel have
not been established. Start bring-up at a low SPI rate; qualify higher rates
with scope measurements and CRC error counters across voltage/temperature.
Neither reducing frequency nor adding a buffer alone proves full throughput:
the measured sustainable rate must also pass the model and workload below.

The MCP itself is limited to FSCK <= 0.85*(FSYSCLK/2), hence 17 MHz with
SYSCLK=40 MHz [1,2]. Do not use 20 MHz based on an old front-page expectation.
Use some clock tolerance margin below the 17 MHz boundary; 16 MHz is a
candidate only after the end-to-end timing is closed. If SYSCLK is divided
to 20 MHz, the corresponding SPI ceiling falls to 8.5 MHz.

F02 - HIGH: Only J1 pin 39 provides a host-ground connection.

J1 ground pins 6,9,14,20,25,30,34 are explicitly unconnected in both saved
files. SPI uses distant signal pins and long branches, with no nearby header
return conductors. This increases return-loop area and sensitivity to harness
inductance and simultaneous switching. Connect all these designated ground
pins to the board ground plane with short connections. Carry the ground
conductors through the actual mating cable/adapter, especially near SPI pins.
The existing In1.Cu ground plane is useful but does not fix the missing
connector return paths.

F03 - HIGH, CONDITIONAL: Inputs can be driven while the PCB is unpowered.

J6 powers the PCB independently; J1 power pins are not used. Jetson SCK/MOSI
drive MCP input pins through 33 ohms, without board-side power-off isolation.
The MCP controller's other pins are limited to VDD+0.3 V [1, section 9.1].
A live Jetson driving 3.3 V into VDD=0 V is outside that stated input range.
Do not assume 33 ohms provides safe injection-current limiting. Resolve the
power-sequencing contract, including unplugged J6, brownouts and restarts.
Where independent power loss is permitted, use appropriate power-off-tolerant
buffering/isolation and a defined enable/power-good scheme. Verify the
opposite power sequence at the host too. This is a conditional exposure;
there is no claim that an unpowered-input test has been performed.

F04 - MEDIUM: Bank inputs lack explicit board-side bias.

J1.32/GPIO_7 and J1.31/GPIO_11 go directly to CMOS logic without board-side
bias resistors. The carrier table gives GPIO07 a default 'z' state and GPIO11
a default pull-down [3, Table 3-3]. Thus it is inaccurate to assume both are
defined by the carrier at all times. Provide a defined boot/reset/unplugged
state, normally with weak pulls and verified pinmux. Because of the TXB0108,
TI requires external pulls greater than 50 kohm [4, section 7.3.5]; a value
such as 100 kohm is a candidate, not proof it overrides every host condition.
Native CS pulls R34-R37 are already 100 kohm, which follows this guidance.
Both native CS high deselect all controllers regardless of bank value, but
floating logic inputs and selection transitions still need defined behavior.

F05 - MEDIUM: VDD bypass returns do not follow Microchip's preferred layout.

All eight VDD pin-14 bypass capacitors are 2.425 mm pad-center to pad-center
from the corresponding supply pin. That distance alone does not validate the
decoupling loop. Pin 22 and capacitor ground terminals use separate local
connections into the ground plane; the copper-path audit found no direct
surface return between them. AN4560 explicitly recommends a direct low-
impedance pin-22-to-bypass return and warns about relying only on a plane
connection [5, page 4]. Review/reroute these loops using that topology, with
ground vias near the capacitor. This is an EMC/layout recommendation gap,
not evidence of missing DC ground or a guaranteed functional failure.

F06 - MEDIUM: Oscillator network is plausible, not yet qualified.

Y1-Y8 use the exact Abracon 40 MHz crystal named in AN4560. Active crystal
pads, grounded case pads and two load capacitors are correctly connected.
Oscillator tracks remain on the top layer without signal vias. However,
the modeled planar OSC1 path is about 11.7 mm versus about 4.5-4.8 mm for
OSC2; the routed loop is less compact than the crystal's proximity suggests.
Shorten these routes and minimize loop area and nearby aggressors.

The selected crystal has CL=18 pF; two 27 pF caps give 13.5 pF before effective
stray capacitance, requiring about 4.5 pF effective stray for that load.
AN4560 reports testing this crystal with two 18 pF external capacitors [5].
That example does not by itself establish the right value on this PCB.
Evaluate external-cap values, oscillator start-up over voltage/temperature,
frequency and crystal drive. The crystal drive rating is 100 uW maximum [6].
Use a low-capacitance active probe or a buffered clock output; an ordinary
passive probe on OSC1/OSC2 can change the circuit being measured.

F07 - MEDIUM: SOT-23-5 LDO and fuse need a thermal/load qualification.

U3 is the DBV TLV75533PDBVR package, not TI's lower-thermal-resistance DYD
package. Controller maximum current gives 8*20 mA = 160 mA from 3.3 V;
transceiver VIO can add 8*0.5 mA = 4 mA [1]. Budgeting about 180 mA including
logic/pulls gives P_LDO=(5-3.3)*0.18 = 0.306 W. The allowance for logic is an
engineering budget and requires actual load confirmation.

TI's DBV reference boards give 100.8 C/W (EVM) and 231.1 C/W (JEDEC), implying
about 31 C or 71 C temperature rise at 0.306 W [7, Table 5.4]. These are two
reference layouts, not guaranteed bounds on this board. At 70 C ambient the
corresponding junction estimates are about 101 C and 141 C. The 500 mA current
rating is therefore not thermal sign-off. Establish maximum local ambient,
measure the package temperature with the populated board, and improve heat
spreading or regulation if required. Use TI's thermal-metric guidance for
estimating junction temperature from a measured case/top temperature.

C18/C19 are nominal 1 uF X7R and are correctly connected near IN/OUT. TI
requires effective capacitance above 0.47 uF [7]; account for DC bias,
tolerance, temperature and ageing of the actual ordered capacitor. The 10 uF
input bulk and 24 local MCP bypass capacitors exist; supply transient droop
still depends on effective capacitance and the supply cable.

F1 is correctly in series before Q1. At the above 180 mA logic budget,
5 V current is approximately 220 mA with all transceivers recessive, 740 mA
with all dominant using their 70 mA maximum, or 860 mA with all eight in the
specified 85 mA CANH-to-CANL short condition. These are load cases, not
predictions of sustained CAN dominant duty cycle. A regulated 5 V / 2 A
source has sufficient current capacity for these board loads.

The 1 A fuse is not a 1 A precision current limiter. Its archived manufacturer
sheet calls for 25% continuous-current derating and gives 60% of rating at
70 C after temperature derating [8]. Check actual duty, inrush, ambient and
fault clearing; the hottest/high-load case has limited margin. Cold nominal
fuse resistance is 75 milliohms; Q1 is at most 60 milliohms at VGS=-4.5 V,
25 C [8,9]. At 0.74 A those two parts alone drop about 0.10 V, before hot
resistance, copper, connectors and cable. Verify at least 4.5 V at every VCC
pin under the minimum source voltage and transient loads [1, section 9.4].

F08 - MEDIUM: Harness protection and termination are conditional on topology.

Each CAN port has a permanent 120 ohm / 0.25 W termination. This is suitable
for an endpoint with one other termination at the far endpoint. Exactly two
120 ohm terminations give about 60 ohms across an unpowered bus. Disable
terminations on intermediate motors/nodes. Add configurable termination if
ports might be used mid-bus or with already-terminated equipment. Normal
specified 3 V differential drive dissipates 3^2/120=75 mW in each 120 ohm
resistor, below 0.25 W before temperature derating; motor-voltage wiring
faults are a separate protection case.

No external CAN TVS parts or common-mode chokes are populated. Their absence
is not automatically a CAN functional-spec violation. Integrated IC ratings
do not demonstrate whole-robot harness immunity. Define hot-plug, ESD, surge,
ground-offset and motor-noise requirements; select connector-side protection
and test accordingly. D2 is a MOSFET gate-source clamp, not a 5 V input clamp.
J6 requires an already regulated, suitably bounded 5 V source.

The CAN routes are roughly 21-29 mm to their connectors, with about 0.5-7 mm
pair skew. This skew is insignificant relative to a 1 us bit; no evidence
supports adding length-tuning meanders. Closer differential routing and
smaller loop area can improve emissions. Use a suitable twisted-pair harness,
short stubs and a propagation/sample-point budget for its actual length.

F09 - REQUIREMENT MISMATCH: No galvanic isolation in this revision.

All eight CAN grounds, logic ground and J1.39 share one plane, and there are
no isolators or isolated converter. The component list calls this a current
nonisolated V1; the PRD still calls for galvanic isolation. If that requirement
remains active, this design fails it and needs an architectural revision.
If nonisolated operation is the intended accepted scope, update the PRD and
qualify the robot's ground paths and fault exposure explicitly.

F10 - DOCUMENTATION / MECHANICAL ITEMS.

The A4 schematic has channel circuitry outside its page bounds. The ordinary
page export clips it; schematic/full.svg and full.png are expanded review
copies that were inspected. Reorganize into suitable sheets before release.
J1 is a male Harwin header on the bottom side. Verify the mating harness,
pin-1 orientation, clearance above/below the Jetson and cable strain relief.
No mounting holes were found in this PCB. Its 77 x 80 mm size does not prove
mechanical fit or vibration retention in the robot. The component inventory,
assembly drawings and purchase MPNs also need to match this current revision.
Label J6 polarity and the CAN H/L/G pin assignments explicitly in the release
documentation/harness drawing: J6 pin 1 is GND and pin 2 is positive input.
Connector numbering alone is not a universal polarity convention.

2. Correct circuitry verified
------------------------------
U9-U16 have VCC pin 25 on protected 5 V, VDD pin 14 and VIO pin 1 on 3.3 V,
ground pins 22/24 grounded, and STBY pin 5 low. Every TXCAN15 -> TXD23 and
RXD28 -> RXCAN16 link is present; those internal controller/transceiver links
are necessary. TXD is correctly an input in the current saved schematic.
Every controller has its own active-low nCS, interrupt input to U4, crystal,
three 100 nF bypass capacitors and individual 33 ohm SDO source resistor.
CANH/CANL/ground reach connector pins 1/2/3 respectively.

U1/U5 OR gates, U2 inverters and U4 NAND are all supplied/bypassed at 3.3 V.
Their supply ranges and logic levels are compatible [11,12,13]. The unused
inverter input is tied to a rail and its output is unused. Although an old
PRD calls this AND/NOT logic, OR gating is the correct implementation for
the required active-low selects. Do not replace gates merely to match prose.

Selection table (native CS numbers per physical SPI bus):
  bank  CS0  CS1       SPI0 selected       SPI1 selected
   0     0    1           CAN2                CAN6
   0     1    0           CAN3                CAN7
   1     0    1           CAN1                CAN5
   1     1    0           CAN4                CAN8
   x     1    1           none                none
   x     0    0           TWO - forbidden     TWO - forbidden

U4 followed by U2F implements AND(nINT1,...,nINT8). The Jetson GPIO_1/J1.29
is low whenever any controller requests service. The eight push-pull nINT
outputs are separate gate inputs and are not wired together. Keep push-pull
mode or provide each separate gate input a pull-up if open-drain is selected.
Use a level-low host IRQ and drain all pending sources before completion.

The reverse-input topology is correct: J6 positive -> F1 -> Q1 drain; Q1
source -> protected VCC; gate pulled toward ground through R38; D2 cathode
at source and anode at gate [9]. It provides reverse-hookup protection;
do not treat it as guaranteed reverse-current blocking or input overvoltage
regulation. The local 3.3 V output is not tied to the Jetson 3.3 V supply.

Passive/component rating checks: 50 V 100 nF bypass and C0G crystal caps,
25 V 1 uF LDO caps, and 16 V 10 uF bulk have adequate nominal voltage ratings
for the intended 3.3/5 V rails. Effective MLCC capacitance remains a separate
check. The selected 0603 resistor ratings accommodate normal signal/pull
currents: a selected 10 kohm CS pull dissipates about 1.1 mW at 3.3 V and
the 10 kohm gate pull about 2.5 mW at 5 V. Neither resistor sizing nor the
33 ohm SPI resistors make forbidden contention/power-off operation safe.
The local JST XH specification gives 3 A with its specified wire/contact
conditions, above the estimated board supply load; the actual mating contact,
wire gauge and locking/retention still require qualification. Package pad
numbering and supply/ground connections were checked; this is not a supplier
stock or manufacturing-process certification.

PCB: four layers, 1.6 mm, nominal 35 um copper. In1.Cu is an essentially
continuous ground plane with no signal tracks. In2.Cu has a 3.3 V pour and
signals. Much of the clocks and SPI1 MOSI are on In2.Cu, separated from In1
by 1.24 mm of core; bottom signals reference the nearby power pour. Review
return-current transitions and power-plane cuts. The stack is not proof of
controlled impedance or high-frequency power/ground coupling. The common
clock/MOSI/MISO nets have approximately 91-155 mm total copper per net;
longest modeled planar J1-to-controller routes are 92 mm. Add vertical-via,
carrier and harness delay when doing timing. See layout_metrics.json.

3. SPI capacity at eight 1 Mbit/s CAN channels
---------------------------------------------
Assumptions: Classical CAN data frames, 11- or 29-bit IDs, no CAN FD BRS,
and four independent CAN controllers on each of TWO physical SPI buses.
The eight CAN channels together have 8 Mbit/s physical line capacity. CAN
is half duplex: do not add another 8 Mbit/s for simultaneous TX and RX.
Error frames, retransmissions, software gaps, polling, mux operations and
interrupt handling must be budgeted separately. If '1 Mbit/s' means CAN FD
arbitration with a faster data phase, this model is not the right workload.

Frame length before bit stuffing, INCLUDING three intermission bits:
  Standard ID: B=47+8*DLC. Extended ID: B=67+8*DLC.
For DLC=8, B=111 or 131 bits; maximum arrival-rate bounds are 9009 or 7634
frames/s/channel. Stuffing only reduces these error-free frame rates.
Four controllers therefore present up to 36,036 or 30,534 frames/s to each
SPI bus; eight channels total up to 72,072 or 61,069 frames/s.

Model SPI byte counts, including command/address/CRC overhead:
  RX8: timestamped object 20 + READ_CRC overhead 5 + one-byte WRITE_CRC
       UINC transaction 6 = 31 bytes/frame, with status costs amortized.
  RX8 example with two extra 4-byte READ_CRC status operations: 49 bytes.
  TX8: object+WRITE_CRC 21 + FIFO advance/request WRITE_CRC 6 + timestamped
       TEF read 17 + TEF advance WRITE_CRC 6 = 50 bytes/frame.
  TX8 example with two extra status operations: 68 bytes/frame.

These are transparent custom-driver models, not measured costs or a claimed
exact trace of stock mcp251xfd. WRITE_SAFE choices, larger writes, extra
IRQ polling, errata retries or fewer status operations change the count.
Burst RAM reads can amortize command overhead further. SPI full-duplex wires
do not double useful capacity for sequential read/write device commands.

Per physical SPI bus, four fully loaded CAN channels, DLC=8:
                                  Standard ID       Extended ID
  RX, status amortized             8.94 Mbit/s        7.57 Mbit/s
  RX, two status reads/frame      14.13 Mbit/s       11.97 Mbit/s
  TX+TEF, status amortized        14.41 Mbit/s       12.21 Mbit/s
  TX+TEF, two status reads/frame  19.60 Mbit/s       16.61 Mbit/s

At 16 MHz, RX31 standard-ID wire occupancy is 55.9%; RX49 is 88.3%.
RX49 leaves only 3.25 us/frame for ALL nonclock overhead at the upper-bound
frame rate. TX68 standard-ID is already above 17 MHz before software gaps.
An efficient RX/TX implementation may be feasible; arbitrary message
direction mixes and inefficient transactions are not automatically feasible.
At 10 MHz, RX31 standard-ID already consumes 89.4%, leaving little reserve;
RX49 cannot meet the upper-bound offered load. This makes F01 material.

Short frames stress the design more than an '8 Mbit/s total' comparison:
standard-ID DLC0 has a 47-bit no-stuff frame and a theoretical 170,213 frames/s
across eight channels. A custom 12-byte RX-header read with CRC+UINC costs
23 bytes, demanding 15.66 Mbit/s per SPI bus before status reads/gaps. Adding
two status reads gives 41 bytes and 27.91 Mbit/s. Even an intentionally
overgenerous 55-bit frame length (maximum stuff-bit bound) gives 23.85 Mbit/s
for that unbatched model, above the controller's ceiling. A driver reading
full fixed-size eight-byte FIFO slots for DLC0 costs more than a header-only
model. A conservative no-stuff upper bound exceeding capacity is not alone
proof of real overload; the 55-bit case shows the inefficient model has a
problem even allowing maximum stuffing. General loss-free saturation for
all frame sizes/directions is not established by this architecture review.

Use actual ID, DLC, direction, update rate, CAN utilization and driver
transactions to set a supported operating envelope. bandwidth.json/.csv and
verify_design.py contain the reproducible model for DLC 0,1,2,4,8.

4. Jetson capacity, driver requirements and robot example
--------------------------------------------------------
The Jetson offers the two SPI interfaces needed by this topology [3]. Its
host-memory bandwidth is far above the few MB/s exchanged here; the relevant
risks are electrical timing and kernel transaction/interrupt latency. At
72,072 frames/s, one 1.7 GHz core provides about 23,587 cycles/frame before
other work. A 10,000-cycle/frame implementation alone would consume about
42% of one such core; 20,000 cycles would consume about 85%. These are
arithmetic examples, not driver benchmarks. CPU clock, core count, AI TOPS
and nominal SPI clock cannot prove worst-case real-time throughput.

The answer for the Jetson is therefore: no raw memory-bandwidth obstacle is
apparent, and the required SPI topology exists; sustained loss-free service
still requires a measured efficient kernel implementation and timing closure.
No Jetson was connected for this review. The requested SPI clock must be
verified on the pins; a device-tree clock setting is not a rate measurement.
Both physical SPI buses must make progress concurrently. Serializing all
eight controllers behind one global blocking worker wastes that opportunity.

Current upstream mcp251xfd explicitly supports microchip,mcp251863, applies
CRC quirks and requests IRQF_SHARED | IRQF_ONESHOT [10]. That is a useful
reference; it is not evidence those features exist in the installed JetPack
kernel. Reuse/backport proven pieces where appropriate. Board-specific mux,
shared IRQ and scheduling behavior still need implementation and validation.

For each physical bus, bank GPIO ownership must be serialized with BOTH
native CS paths. Change bank only while both CS are high, after the previous
SDO release. The inspected spi-mux driver binds a mux to one parent native
chip select; a single instance is not a ready-made four-child representation
of this board. A possible solution is two 2-state virtual muxes sharing one
properly serialized bank-control provider, or a board-specific SPI wrapper.
Validate this with the actual kernel locking and message queue behavior.
Do not toggle bank independently from userspace or race direct GPIO writes
against the SPI framework. The archived PRD amendment already supersedes
the old assertion that GPIO registers must be accessed outside the kernel.

At the MCP pins [1, Table 9-7], nCS setup >= T_SCK/2; nCS hold from the last
rising SCK >= T_SCK; inactive gap >= T_SCK; SDO high-Z can take 2*T_SCK.
At 16 MHz these are 31.25, 62.5, 62.5 and 125 ns respectively. Add translator,
OR/inverter, route and host timing when programming master-side intervals.
Native CS passes one OR; bank inversion can add another gate. Enforce the
SDO-release gap on every change of selected controller, including native-CS
changes without a bank change. A fixed 300 ns gap is a possible bring-up
margin at 16 MHz, not a valid universal constant at lower clock rates.
The spi-mux path also needs verification that parent CS setup/hold settings
are preserved/applied; it copies basic mode/speed settings, not every timing
field in the inspected implementation.

Errata DS80000984B [2] must inform custom code:
  - CRC-protected affected reads, bounded retry and fault reporting.
  - RX FIFO index sanity checks / multiple reads: FIFOCI corruption can have
    a matching CRC. CRC alone is not enough for this erratum.
  - ECC correction is not trustworthy in all cases: detect/report/recover.
  - Avoid invalid SFR rollover accesses and the IOCON multi-byte write issue.
  - Respect the 0.85 clock ratio and valid-FIFO-before-RAM-read sequencing.
  - Sleep/wake needs the specified 50-SYSCLK-cycle timing (1.25 us at
    40 MHz) and two successful OSCRDY reads; retries must be bounded.
  - Avoid spurious CS pulses and handle CRC format-error indication.

A 32-object RX FIFO at the upper-bound 9009 frames/s fills in about 3.55 ms.
Actual FIFO allocation, interrupt threshold and reserve must be explicit;
the 2 KB RAM also holds TX and completion objects. Do not assume the full
2 KB is one RX queue. Measure worst service stalls, including simultaneous
activity on other controllers, AI workloads, power management and bus faults.

Illustrative workload from the PRD's approximately 25 motors, NOT an assumed
motor protocol: one eight-byte command and one eight-byte feedback at 1 kHz
per motor gives 50,000 CAN frames/s. Before stuffing, total CAN occupancy is
69.4% for standard IDs or 81.9% for extended IDs, averaged across eight buses.
A bus with four motors is already 88.8% standard-ID or 104.8% extended-ID.
Thus four extended-ID motors cannot each exchange that command+feedback pair
at 1 kHz on a 1 Mbit/s bus, even before stuffing/retries. Standard-ID traffic
at that density also has little margin. The CAN segment can be the bottleneck.

With this example evenly spread over the two SPI buses and 50% RX/50% TX,
SPI demand is about 8.10 Mbit/s/bus with status amortization, or 11.70 Mbit/s
with the two-status-read model, before gaps. Real channel grouping is discrete
and must be checked per bus. Obtain the actual motor update/feedback policy,
ID format and DLC before defining the supported robot control rate.

At SYSCLK=40 MHz a possible 1 Mbit/s nominal bit-time is BRP=1, 40 time quanta,
TSEG1=31, TSEG2=8, sample point 80%, SJW<=8. Encoded register fields subtract
one where specified (BRP=0, NTSEG1=30, NTSEG2=7). This is a starting setting;
the selected sample point/SJW must fit the actual cable/oscillator budget.

5. Release criteria
--------------------
Before releasing a production PCB: resolve F01-F06 and the active isolation
requirement, reconcile the BOM/sheets, establish thermal/environment limits,
and verify connector mechanics and fabricator rules. The highest-value board
changes are ground returns, defined startup states, power-sequence handling
and tighter local bypass/oscillator loops. Final buffer/protection choices
depend on the cable and power contract; they were not guessed into the CAD.

On prototypes, run all eight channels at 1 Mbit/s simultaneously with the
actual harness. Exercise RX-heavy, TX-heavy and mixed traffic, standard and
extended IDs, short and eight-byte frames, bursts and controller switches.
Record sequence loss, CRC/retry counts, FIFO/TEF overflow, CAN errors/bus-off,
CPU load and tail latency under the intended AI/robot workload. Capture SCK,
MISO, CS and bank transitions at the devices and verify power/clock behavior
over the specified voltage and temperature range. Test both power sequences,
brownout, cable faults and the defined EMC disturbances. A pass needs explicit
duration, load envelope and zero unexplained loss; none was measured here.

7. Update for 5 cm cable, RobStride motors and IMUs
--------------------------------------------------
User-provided inputs: Jetson-to-board cable approximately 5 cm; CAN nodes
mostly RobStride motors and IMUs. The exact motor models, firmware/protocol
mode, motor count per segment, command/feedback rates and IMU frame schedule
remain unspecified. No CAD changes were made for this clarification.

Manufacturer evidence: the RobStride04 manual, English section 4 on PDF page
46 (printed page 44), specifies Classical CAN at 1 Mbit/s with 29-bit IDs and
eight-byte data. The operation command on PDF pages 47-48 returns a motor
feedback frame. This supports the report's extended-ID workload for the
private protocol; it does not establish every model/firmware or alternate
protocol mode. Vendor source:
https://www.robstride.com/assets/product_manual_robStride04-37549d59.pdf
Archived as sources/RobStride04_Manual.pdf, with its hash in the manifest.

At a nominal cable propagation speed of 2e8 m/s, the 5 cm cable contributes
about 0.25 ns in each direction, or 0.5 ns SCK-out/MISO-return flight time.
That is a small addition to the 34.4 ns worst-case device/translator path;
ordinary half-cycle sampling at 16 MHz remains unclosed. This flight-time
estimate is not a cable specification or a load model. Connect J1's available
grounds and use nearby ground conductors in the short harness. Cable length
alone does not establish impedance, capacitance, grounding or sample margin.

For ONE 1 Mbit/s CAN segment, one command plus one feedback per update,
private-protocol eight-byte extended frames, and NO IMU/extra traffic:

  Motors on bus   Per-motor rate   CAN occupancy, no stuffing to upper bound
       2             500 Hz                     26.2% - 32.0%
       2            1000 Hz                     52.4% - 64.0%
       3             500 Hz                     39.3% - 48.0%
       3            1000 Hz                     78.6% - 96.0%
       4             500 Hz                     52.4% - 64.0%
       4            1000 Hz                    104.8% - 128.0%

The upper bound uses 160 bits/frame, including worst-case stuffing and
intermission; actual stuffing depends on IDs, payload and CRC. Three motors
at 1 kHz have little worst-case reserve; four at that rate cannot fit.
At 500 Hz the displayed motor-only scenarios leave more room, but the
remaining capacity is not automatically all available for scheduled IMUs:
also budget faults, configuration, retransmissions and latency margin.

IMU models matter because one measurement may require multiple CAN frames.
For example, 1000 extended eight-byte IMU frames/s consume another 13.1%
before stuffing, up to 16.0% with the conservative stuffing bound, on whichever
CAN segment carries them. This is an illustrative frame rate, not an assumed
IMU format or sample rate. Sum actual frame rates and lengths per segment:

  utilization = sum(frames_per_second * on_wire_bits_per_frame) / 1,000,000.

For four such CAN segments on one SPI bus, the extended-ID SPI demand in
section 3 is the applicable estimate: 7.57 Mbit/s for RX with status amortized,
11.97 Mbit/s for RX with two status reads/frame, 12.21 Mbit/s for TX+completion
with status amortized, or 16.61 Mbit/s for the extra-status TX model, before
nonclock gaps. Actual mixed direction and motor update rates will change
these values. A 5 cm cable does not change the number of SPI bytes needed.
