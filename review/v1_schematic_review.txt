V1 schematic review - 2026-10-18

Reviewed all eight screenshots in v1screenshots and the saved schematic
breakout/breakout.kicad_sch, not just the three pictured CAN controllers.
This review treats the drawing as the common-ground, nonisolated version
discussed most recently. The older isolated component list is not the current
electrical reference for this drawing. No schematic or project files changed.

RESULT: several definite wiring errors remain. Do not power this version from
the Jetson or release a PCB from it until the power and connectivity errors
below are corrected. This is a schematic review; routing, thermal behavior,
power sequencing and real signal integrity remain to be checked on the layout
and prototype.

1. CAN power rails are reversed relative to the intended 3.3 V interface.

Screenshot ss4 and the netlist establish that J6 -> F2 feeds VDD_VIO directly;
U3 takes that rail as input and generates VCC. With a 5 V input, VDD_VIO is
therefore 5 V and VCC is 3.3 V. All eight CAN chips take controller VDD pin 14
and transceiver VIO pin 1 from the former, and transceiver VCC pin 25 from the
latter. MCP251863 Figure 1-2 instead specifies this arrangement:

  Fused/protected +5 V -> every MCP251863 VCC pin 25 and U3 IN/EN
  U3 3.3 V output     -> every MCP251863 VDD pin 14 and VIO pin 1
                      -> U1, U2, U4, U5 supply pins
                      -> every CAN CS pull-up
  Common GND         -> every MCP251863 pin 22 and pin 24, U3 and logic GND

Use explicit +5V and +3V3 net names. Do not only exchange the existing label
text: the logic ICs are already on the LDO output and must stay there, while
the CAN supply pins and CS pull-ups need their connections changed.

The present 3.3 V CAN VCC is below its 4.5 V operating minimum. The present
5 V controller VDD also makes SDO a 5 V-domain output connected to the
Jetson, and gives digital inputs a 3.5 V minimum HIGH threshold. A 33 ohm
series resistor is not voltage translation or protection against that error.
Sources: MCP251863 DS20006624B, Figure 1-2, Tables 9-2 and 9-8.

2. SPI clock and the division into two buses are not connected correctly.

All eight SCK pins 9 are on the global net SCK. Neither SPI0_SCK nor SPI1_SCK
is connected to SCK. Those two named nets end at their header resistors and
test points. Consequently none of the controllers receives a Jetson clock.

All eight SDI pins 10 use SPI0_MOSI. All eight SDO pins 11 return through their
individual 33 ohm resistors to SPI0_MISO. SPI1_MOSI and SPI1_MISO have no CAN
controllers attached. This also permits independently selected devices on the
two intended buses to drive the same MISO line.

Connect CAN1-CAN4 (U9-U12) to SPI0_SCK/MOSI/MISO. Connect CAN5-CAN8 (U13-U16)
to SPI1_SCK/MOSI/MISO. Retain one individual SDO resistor near each MCP251863.
Do not connect the two physical SPI clocks or the two MISO buses together.

3. Three CAN controllers share CAN7_CS due to copied labels.

Actual U13/CAN5, U14/CAN6 and U15/CAN7 pin 13 all connect to CAN7_CS.
Their R17, R24 and R27 pull-ups are consequently also on that same net.
U5's CAN5_CS and CAN6_CS outputs reach only their test points.

Change U13 pin 13 / R17 to CAN5_CS, U14 pin 13 / R24 to CAN6_CS, and keep
U15 pin 13 / R27 on CAN7_CS. U16 / CAN8_CS is distinct already.
As drawn, selecting CAN7 can enable three SDO drivers at once.

4. Add the LDO's specified local input and output capacitors.

U3 has only a 100 nF local input capacitor and no dedicated output capacitor.
TI specifies nominal 1 uF or larger at IN and OUT, and requires effective
capacitance greater than 0.47 uF after derating. Fit a suitable ceramic 1 uF
or larger from pin 1 to GND and another from pin 5 to GND, immediately at U3.
Keep the existing 100 nF if useful; distributed CAN/gate decouplers do not
replace the specified local regulator capacitors. Pin 3 EN tied to input is
valid for always-on operation; pin 4 NC may be left open.

Add input bulk capacitance, for example 10 uF at the protected 5 V entry, and
appropriate rail bulk near the CAN groups. Keep each existing 100 nF directly
at its respective VDD, VIO or VCC pin with a short ground return. Additional
local 1 uF at a CAN port/group is a practical starting option, to be checked
against supply impedance and simultaneous activity rather than treated as a
mandatory per-pin value from the MCP datasheet.
Source: TLV755P Rev. D, pin functions and sections 5.3, 7.1.1 and 7.3.

5. Unused inverter inputs and startup controls need defined states.

U2E input pin 11 and U2F input pin 13 are marked no-connect. They are CMOS
inputs, not NC pins. Tie unused inputs to GND or 3.3 V; their unused outputs
pins 10 and 12 can remain open. If using U2E to invert IRQ below, only U2F
remains unused.

There is no board-side bias on the four native Jetson CS inputs or the two
bank GPIOs. The eight pull-ups on the CAN-side CS outputs cannot establish
the inputs of a powered push-pull OR gate. Establish native CS HIGH and a
defined bank value during startup, including before software configures pins.

Choose the Jetson-facing bias with the actual carrier's level translators in
mind. NVIDIA's carrier specification Table 3-3 note 3 identifies TXB0108
translators and weak drive on these signals. TI recommends external pulls
above 50 kohm; 100 kohm is a possible starting value requiring leakage,
internal-pull and logic-margin checks. Do not automatically use the CAN-side
10 kohm resistor value on Jetson-driven nets. The 10 kohm CAN CS pull-ups are
behind the strong OR outputs, so that is a different load case.
Sources: Diodes 74LVC04A Rev. 5-2 p3 note 5; NVIDIA carrier specification
SP-11324-001_v1.3 Table 3-3 note 3; TXB0108 Rev. L sections 7.3.5 and 8.2.2.

6. Bank selection gates are logically valid; channel order is unusual.

The actual OR/NOT network implements, for each SPI bus:

  /CS1 = /nativeCS0 OR NOT(bank)
  /CS2 = /nativeCS0 OR bank
  /CS3 = /nativeCS1 OR bank
  /CS4 = /nativeCS1 OR NOT(bank)

Here the second bus substitutes CAN5-CAN8 for CAN1-CAN4. The selected logical
outputs are:

  Bank   Active native CS   SPI0 controller   SPI1 controller
  0      CS0                CAN2              CAN6
  0      CS1                CAN3              CAN7
  1      CS0                CAN1              CAN5
  1      CS1                CAN4              CAN8

This is a valid four-device selection mapping after item 3 is fixed. Change
gate connections only if you want sequential bank numbering; otherwise use
this mapping in the device tree/mux design. Both native CS HIGH deselect all
four logical outputs. Never drive both native CS LOW. Change bank only while
both native CS are HIGH and the previous device has released MISO. Separate
bank GPIOs on J1 pins 32 and 31 are appropriate for independent SPI buses.
The netlist-derived truth tables are saved in audit_results.json.

At 3.0-3.6 V, 50 pF and -40 to +125 C the selected OR has a 5 ns maximum
delay, and the NOT has 6 ns maximum. The native CS path is one OR; the longest
bank-to-CS path is one NOT plus one OR, approximately 11 ns by summing these
specified stage limits. They suit the intended MHz operation. They do not
eliminate the need to configure CS timing at the MCP pins.

At an initial 8 MHz SPI clock, Table 9-7 requires 62.5 ns CS-to-first-SCK setup,
125 ns from the relevant final SCK rising edge to CS rising, and allows up to
250 ns for SDO to become high impedance after deselection. Account for gate,
PCB and host timing: e.g. target at least 100 ns native-CS setup and a 300 ns
inter-device deselection gap as starting margins, then verify actual waveforms.
The relevant SCK-to-CS hold requirement must be met at the MCP pins as well.
Maximum SPI is 17 MHz with a 40 MHz SYSCLK (0.85 * SYSCLK/2), not 40 MHz.
Software bank ownership, bus serialization and exact JetPack mux support are
not established by this schematic review.
Sources: 74LVC32A Rev. 11 Table 7; 74LVC04A p5; MCP251863 Table 9-7.

7. Shared IRQ NAND is usable with HIGH interrupt polarity.

U4 is a NAND: all eight nINT inputs HIGH gives output LOW; any LOW input gives
output HIGH. Its direct GPIO_1 connection therefore calls for a LEVEL_HIGH
interrupt configuration. It is not an active-low combined IRQ. Alternatively
insert spare U2E (pin 11 input, pin 10 output) to obtain a LEVEL_LOW IRQ. Do
not configure an edge-only interrupt for the aggregate, because another source
can assert while the line is already active.

The eight inputs are separate, so push-pull controller IRQ outputs are not
shorted together. With the power rails corrected, nINT outputs are in the
3.3 V controller domain. Optional 10 kohm pull-ups at each nINT give a defined
idle level when outputs are high impedance and support future open-drain
configuration; push-pull operation itself does not require those pull-ups.
U4's physical pin allocation, supply pins, bypass and NC9/10/13 are correct.
Its maximum 3.3 V-domain, 50 pF delay is 15.5 ns over -40 to +125 C.

Current upstream mcp251xfd was inspected and requests IRQF_SHARED | IRQF_ONESHOT;
its handler starts with IRQ_NONE and checks its controller's pending flags.
That is evidence for shared IRQ support, not proof about the JetPack kernel
you will install. Verify that exact driver supports sharing and that all eight
device nodes use the same physical IRQ with consistent polarity. Inversion
alone does not address an older driver's missing shared-IRQ support. Leave
individual nINT test access and consider individual-IRQ routing options for
bring-up. No driver was built or tested here.
Sources: 74AHC_AHCT30 Rev. 8 Tables 4 and 8; upstream mcp251xfd-core.c.

8. Input reverse protection is correctly oriented but not fully specified.

F2 is in series and D2 is in parallel, with cathode at fused positive input
and anode at GND. This polarity is correct for the requested shunt topology.
However D2 is a generic D_Schottky with no selected manufacturer part number
or footprint. Resolve that before a manufacturing BOM is generated.

A 2 A-limited source may never open the selected 2 A fuse. The diode must
survive actual source current until shutdown/clearing, indefinitely if no
clearing occurs. Also a Schottky clamp can leave the load at -VF, not 0 V.
TLV755 input/EN and MCP power pins have -0.3 V absolute minima, so confirm
guaranteed reverse-fault voltage at the loads, including cold diode drop and
transients. The previously proposed STPS20L15D is not a guaranteed sub-0.3 V
clamp over all fault conditions. A fuse/diode symbol pair alone does not prove
complete reverse-input protection. A reverse-blocking arrangement or a
qualified fault-tolerant power path may still be needed.
The original TPS259530 eFuse is absent; this drawing relies on the fuse and
source for fault current handling. An eFuse is not inherently mandatory for
the new nonisolated architecture, but its old electronic current-limit and
inrush functions are absent too.
Sources: TLV755P absolute maximum ratings; MCP251863 section 9.1;
Littelfuse 466 and STPS20L15 datasheets archived in datasheets/.

9. CAN connectors currently have no external TVS, and fixed termination.

There are no ESDONCAN1LT1G parts or equivalent CAN-line protection devices in
the saved netlist. Add the selected protection footprint at each external CAN
connector: pins 1/2 to CANH/CANL and pin 3 to the common board ground, with a
short return. For the chosen bidirectional dual protector either line may use
pin 1 or 2. Its transient ratings and clamping must suit the actual exposure;
this is not a claim of protection against arbitrary sustained 48 V faults.

Each port has a permanently connected 120 ohm resistor. That is appropriate
when the board is guaranteed to be one physical endpoint of each separate
bus. Otherwise add a solder jumper or other disconnect in series. Each bus
needs exactly two end terminations, including any inside actuators. Eight
independent CAN buses may each have their own board termination.

10. Crystals are wired correctly, but the fitted value needs reconciliation.

Every Y1-Y8 has active pads 1 and 3 across OSC1/OSC2, ground pads 2 and 4 to
ground, and a shunt capacitor on each active node. These connections are right.
The schematic uses 27 pF for C2-C17, while the selected component list says
18 pF. This is a real assembly-document mismatch, not proof that 27 pF is wrong.

For equal external capacitors, CL = C/2 + Cstray. With an 18 pF target and
4.5 pF effective stray, C = 27 pF. Microchip reports that this exact Abracon
crystal worked with 18 pF external capacitors on its evaluation boards. Either
starting population needs PCB-specific startup/frequency/drive checks. Choose
and document the intended initial value and matching C0G/NP0 MPN; use pF, not
uF. Keep crystal traces and return paths short. Optional series/feedback
resistor provisions can be considered if layout space permits.
Source: MCP251863 Board Design Hints, section 1 and Figure 2-1.

11. LDO current rating is plausible; verify its actual thermal layout.

The CAN controller table lists 20 mA maximum VDD supply current at 40 MHz,
so eight controllers account for 160 mA, plus up to roughly 4 mA total VIO
under listed dominant-state conditions and the gates/bias/dynamic load.
The corrected 3.3 V load is within this LDO's 500 mA output rating in principle.
At a 200 mA design allowance, its dissipation is (5 - 3.3)*0.2 = 0.34 W.
The higher CAN VCC current bypasses the LDO after item 1 is corrected.

Do not use the new thermally enhanced DYD-package figure for the selected
PDBVR/DBV part. TI gives DBV RthetaJA of 231.1 C/W on its JEDEC board and
100.8 C/W on its EVM example. Thus 0.34 W corresponds to approximately 79 C
or 34 C rise in those example conditions, not a universal board prediction.
Check copper, ambient, actual load and junction margin. An LDO remains a
reasonable choice; a switcher is not required solely by this voltage ratio.
Sources: MCP251863 Tables 9-2 and 9-8; TLV755P sections 5.3, 5.4 and 7.1.5.

12. CAD/ERC cleanup is needed, including a supplied-symbol defect.

KiCad CLI ERC found 125 reported violations: 37 errors and 88 warnings.
Counts by type are 76 footprint-library warnings, 26 unconnected header pins,
12 symbol/library mismatch warnings, 8 output/output conflicts, 2 undriven
power nets, and 1 undriven input (the disconnected SCK net).
These are not 125 independent hardware faults.

The bundled MCP251863 symbol incorrectly declares pin 23 TXD as an output.
The manufacturer defines TXD as INPUT. Pin 15 TXCAN -> pin 23 TXD is correct
and is present on all eight devices; do not remove those wires to silence ERC.
Fix the library's TXD electrical type and update the schematic's cached symbol
carefully, preserving the user's symbol/graphics changes. Pin 28 RXD is
correctly an output and connects correctly to pin 16 RXCAN, an input.
The TXD type error in the previously supplied asset causes all eight reported
output/output conflicts. No physical pin-number correction is needed there.

There are 72 components without footprint assignments: the 16 crystal load
capacitors, 32 resistors, generic D2, and 23 test point symbols. Assign actual
packages, including at least the selected 1206 quarter-watt CAN termination
resistors and chosen crystal capacitor size.

breakout/fp-lib-table is missing. The CLI cannot resolve BreakoutBoard
footprints in its current configuration. Register the existing local library
at project level; the provided kicad_footprints/fp-lib-table template already
uses ${KIPRJMOD}/../kicad_footprints/BreakoutBoard.pretty for this project path.
Review the symbol/library mismatches before updating symbols globally.
Add PWR_FLAG declarations for genuinely externally powered input/ground nets
after fixing their wiring. Mark unused header signal/power pins with NC flags.

J1's SPI signal pin assignments match the supplied Jetson pinout. Ground is
currently connected only on pin 39; also connect the available header ground
pins 6, 9, 14, 20, 25, 30 and 34 to improve nearby SPI return paths. Do not join
the local 3.3 V regulator output to Jetson 3.3 V just to add those grounds.
Use a short interconnect. Check the carrier TXB0108 capacitive-load limit when
driving four CAN inputs and cable/PCB capacitance; buffering SCK/MOSI can be
appropriate if the fanout/interconnect exceeds the margin. Confirm power
sequencing prevents driving unpowered Jetson or MCP inputs above their limits.

Correct connections checked on every channel: grounded STBY pin 5 for normal
mode; both ground pins; TXCAN/TXD and RXD/RXCAN links; crystal active/case
connections; main nINT pin 19; and CANH/CANL to connector pins 1/2 respectively.
Unused dedicated interrupt/GPIO pins 6/7 may remain externally unconnected if
their software configuration is suitable. Both independent bank GPIOs and
one bypass capacitor per gate package are already present.
